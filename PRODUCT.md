# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Non-technical founders and small-team operators who are shopping for a freelance or contract builder. They land cold (link in a DM, a referral, a search result) and decide in under thirty seconds whether to take the meeting. They are not engineers; they evaluate trust signals (clarity, taste, evidence of shipped work) before they evaluate stack fit. The job-to-be-done on the page is *"figure out fast whether this person can be trusted with my project, then make contact frictionless."*

## Product Purpose

Win inbound contract and consulting work for Thomas Burridge by being the leave-behind that converts cold readers into qualified intros. Success is measured by inbound contact volume from people the owner has never spoken to, not by traffic, peer applause, or pageviews. Every section earns its place against that goal: if it does not build trust or shorten the path to "let's talk," it should not be on the page.

## Brand Personality

Trusted senior operator. Calm, low-flash, evidence-led. Three words: *grounded, considered, capable.* Voice is first person, plain, direct; the kind of writing a senior engineer uses to explain a system to a smart client over coffee. Not "rockstar," not "ninja," not "passionate about cutting-edge." No exclamation points. No restated headings. The site should feel like the person behind it has nothing to prove and is choosing to show this much, not stretching to fill the space.

## Anti-references

Actively avoid the **generic dev portfolio** lane. Concrete failures to refuse:

- Cool grey neutrals plus a single neon accent (the default shadcn-on-Tailwind look).
- Terminal-styled hero with a typing-cursor tagline.
- A 3D globe on the landing for decoration, when the work has no global story.
- Identical project cards in a 2-up or 3-up grid, each with icon plus title plus three lines.
- Skill matrices, percentage bars, "tech I love" badge clouds.
- Marketing-template cliches: gradient CTAs, hero metric template, testimonials in cards, agency-speak ("we build exceptional digital experiences").

The current site contains several of these by accident. They are the first things to retire.

## Design Principles

1. **Earn trust before showing off.** A founder evaluating a freelancer wants reliability proof, not entertainment. Lead with shipped work and clear outcomes; let craft show through restraint, not through flash.
2. **Show the work, not the worker.** Projects are the proof; the bio is supporting cast. Each project should communicate *problem, what shipped, result* in a way a non-engineer can parse in fifteen seconds.
3. **Take a visual position.** The default dev-portfolio palette is camouflage. Pick a register and commit to it; "safe" reads as junior.
4. **Quiet over loud.** Senior tone is fewer words, more whitespace, slower motion, single deliberate accents. If a section needs a banner to feel important, the section is not important enough.
5. **Make the ask obvious.** The path from "interested" to "in your inbox" should be one visible step from any screen. Hidden contact pages cost leads.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for body and interactive states.
- Full keyboard reachability and visible focus rings on every actionable element.
- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for non-essential animation; no parallax or auto-playing motion that the reader cannot stop.
- Body type sized so that 65 to 75ch lines hold at default zoom; no fixed pixel sizes that break at 200% zoom.
- Color is never the sole carrier of meaning (status, error, link).
